For every test

X0 Y0 is the front-left corner of the test. Labels burn at the label power and feed.

Material grid

Power across, feed up the side: find how dark an engraving gets, or which cell scores cleanly.

Focus ladder

The same line at a row of heights: the thinnest, sharpest one is in focus. Engraving wants the thinnest line; cutting often goes a little lower.

Cut-through

Short cuts at one power, feed up the side and passes across: the first one through is your cut setting.

How the offset works

  • The laser sits beside the spindle. grblHAL’s spindle offset plugin keeps that distance in $770 (X) and $771 (Y), in mm.
  • Switching to the laser (M104 Q1 for spindle 1) moves the head by the offset, so the laser lands where the spindle’s tip was. Switching back moves it back. The move happens at the current height, so Laser Toolbox goes to Safe Z first.
  • With $772=1 (Update G92 on spindle change) X0 Y0 stays where it was, so a laser program lands where the same program would with the spindle. With 0, work coordinates move by the offset instead.
  • Selecting the laser again while it’s selected moves the head back (grblHAL counts it as switching away). So Laser Toolbox’s programs never select a spindle: select the laser first, with ncSender’s Laser Mode or the Switch buttons.

The crosshair

  • Many modules (the LaserTree K60 among them) have a red crosshair, a positioning laser, that shows while the module has power and goes dark while the beam fires. It sits a little off the beam; its offset is kept in Laser Toolbox (grblHAL has one slot, used by the spindle).
  • Set X0 Y0 at the crosshair: put the crosshair on a stock corner and press it. Work zero then sits there for the spindle and the laser alike, with nothing fired.
  • With the module on a relay (Mist, Flood or an aux output), the buttons and programs here can switch it.

The tests

  • Material: power across, feed up the side. For engraving, pick the cell with the shade you want, fastest first. For scoring, the cleanest line.
  • Focus: the same line at stepped heights. The thinnest line is in focus; keep that Z (and Z0 where it was) for real jobs.
  • Cut-through: at full power, rows of feed and columns of passes. The first cut that drops through is the setting; a little slower for margin.
  • Every program names the power mode to set by hand (the K60’s 20, 40 or 60 W) in its header, and Laser Toolbox says it when the program loads. It fires only on cutting moves with M4, and needs grblHAL’s laser mode ($32=1) on.

Safety

  • Wear glasses rated for your laser’s wavelength. Never leave a laser running unattended; keep a fire extinguisher near.
  • With laser mode off, M4 keeps the beam on during rapids. Laser Toolbox won’t make a program until it’s on.
  • Remove the laser, or switch to the spindle, before running the spindle.
